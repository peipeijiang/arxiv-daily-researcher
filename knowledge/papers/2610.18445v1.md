# The Singularity Has a Distribution: Exact Timing Laws and Detection Limits for a Stochastic Intelligence Explosion

[查看原文](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18445v1)

## 一句话结论

> 论文研究AI研究自动化中随机正反馈循环导致的智能爆炸时间分布，推导出爆炸时间的精确分布、检测所需最小样本量以及参数不确定性下的尾部行为，结果表明随机性不改变爆炸阈值但使爆炸时间服从显式分布。

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

自动化人工智能研究在软件能力与研究投入之间形成了一个正反馈循环。我们研究该循环的一种随机实现，其中独立的、正向的、乘性的改进以与当前能力的某个幂成正比的速率到来。在该模型中，随机性使确定性爆炸阈值保持不变，但将爆炸日期替换为一个显式分布。对于等规模改进，爆炸时间是独立指数变量的几何加权和；其$q$-级数表示给出了其变换、矩和尾部行为，包括一个两项的小时间渐近展开。对于Lomax分布的改进规模，爆炸时间恰好服从Gamma分布。自相似性意味着，在参数已知的情况下，关于剩余时间的相对不确定性不会随着爆炸临近而消失。我们构建了一个在恒定想法到达率与几何增长想法到达率之间的固定样本检验。如果基线速率和备择对数速率增量$\ell$已知，则在误差水平$α$下的最小样本量渐近为$(12z_α^2)^{1/3}\ell^{-2/3}$，其中$z_α$是标准正态分布的上$α$分位数。关于增长指数在其临界值附近的不确定性会产生一个带有显式常数的$1/t$尾部，从而导致无限的条件平均等待时间，尽管在正指数下爆炸是确定的。若干独立扩展表明：固定部署延迟在指定的延迟反馈模型中阻止了爆炸；努力的幂律转移会移动增长阈值；并且在确定性监督竞赛中，固定分配份额不会改变渐近机制，除非在指数临界相等时例外。数值示例（并非经验预测）展示了时间安排和可检测性如何依赖于改进的粒度。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Automating AI research creates a positive feedback loop between software capability and research effort. We study a stochastic realisation of this loop in which independent, positive, multiplicative improvements arrive at a rate proportional to a power of current capability. Within this model, randomness leaves the deterministic explosion threshold unchanged but replaces the blow-up date by an explicit distribution. For equal-sized improvements the explosion time is a geometrically weighted sum of independent exponentials; its $q$-series representation gives its transforms, moments and tail behaviour, including a two-term small-time asymptotic. For Lomax-distributed improvement sizes the explosion time is exactly Gamma distributed. Self-similarity implies that, with parameters known, the relative uncertainty about the remaining time does not vanish as explosion approaches. We formulate a fixed-sample test between constant and geometrically increasing idea-arrival rates. If the baseline rate and the alternative log-rate increment $\ell$ are known, the minimum sample size at error level $α$ is asymptotic to $(12z_α^2)^{1/3}\ell^{-2/3}$, where $z_α$ is the upper-$α$ standard normal quantile. Uncertainty about the growth exponent near its critical value produces a $1/t$ tail with an explicit constant, and hence an infinite conditional mean waiting time, although explosion is certain given a positive exponent. Separate extensions show that a fixed deployment delay prevents explosion in the specified delayed-feedback models, that power-law diversion of effort shifts the growth threshold, and that in a deterministic oversight race a fixed allocation share does not change the asymptotic regime, except at a critical equality of exponents. Numerical illustrations, which are not empirical forecasts, show how timing and detectability depend on the granularity of improvements.
