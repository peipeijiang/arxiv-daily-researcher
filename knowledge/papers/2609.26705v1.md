# When are bosonic Gaussian states classical to learn?

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

物理学中的一个基本问题是：经典行为何时从量子系统中涌现？玻色高斯态为探索这一量子—经典边界提供了一个自然的框架，因为它们同时刻画了经典场行为和光的內禀量子特性。在此，我们从学习理论的视角来处理这一问题，提出：玻色高斯态何时在学习意义下是经典的？也就是说，在什么条件下（如果有的话），一个 n 模玻色高斯态可以像学习一个经典 2n 元高斯分布那样，以同样少的样本量和同样简单的操作来学习？我们建立了一个由态的热涨落所支配的学习能力的光滑过渡：

- 冷高斯态在学习意义下是非经典的：当协方差矩阵满足 $Σ\le(\frac12+O(\frac1n))I$，即接近真空协方差时，在单拷贝（即非纠缠）测量下，层析成像从根本上需要 $Ω(n^3)$ 个拷贝，严格超过了学习经典高斯分布的样本复杂度 $Θ(n^2)$。我们证明，即使允许少量拷贝的纠缠测量，这种困难性依然存在。

- 热高斯态在学习意义下是经典的：当热涨落超过真空噪声时，由 $Σ\ge(\frac12+ν)I$ 参数化，其中任意参数 $ν>0$，我们证明单拷贝层析成像需要 $N=Θ\left(n^2\min(n,1+ν^{-1})\right)$ 个拷贝。该界是紧的，并且可以由简单的、非自适应的、非纠缠的外差测量达到。关键的是，对于 $ν=Ω(1)$，样本复杂度降至 $Θ(n^2)$，与经典情形相匹配。

我们的结果紧密刻画了玻色高斯态可学习性中的量子到经典过渡，揭示了基础物理与统计学习理论之间的新颖联系，并对实际传感实验具有启示意义。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

A fundamental question in physics is: When does classical behavior emerge from quantum systems? Bosonic Gaussian states provide a natural setting to explore this quantum-classical boundary, as they capture both the classical field behavior and the intrinsic quantum nature of light. Here, we address this problem from a learning-theoretic perspective by asking: When are bosonic Gaussian states classical to learn? That is, under what conditions (if any) can an n-mode bosonic Gaussian state be learned with as few samples, and with operations as simple, as are needed to learn a classical 2n-variate Gaussian distribution? We establish a smooth crossover in learnability governed by the state's thermal fluctuations:
  - Cold Gaussian states are non-classical to learn: When the covariance matrix satisfies $Σ\le(\frac12+O(\frac1n))I$, i.e. close to the vacuum covariance, tomography under single-copy (i.e., non-entangled) measurements fundamentally requires $Ω(n^3)$ copies, strictly exceeding the sample complexity $Θ(n^2)$ of learning classical Gaussian distributions. We show that this hardness persists even when few-copy entangled measurements are allowed.
  - Warm Gaussian states are classical to learn: When thermal fluctuations exceed the vacuum noise, parameterized by $Σ\ge(\frac12+ν)I$ for any parameter $ν>0$, we prove that single-copy tomography requires $N=Θ\left(n^2\min(n,1+ν^{-1})\right)$ copies. This bound is tight and is achieved by simple, non-adaptive, unentangled heterodyne measurements. Crucially, for $ν=Ω(1)$, the sample complexity drops to $Θ(n^2)$, matching the classical case.
  Our results tightly characterize a quantum-to-classical crossover in the learnability of bosonic Gaussian states, reveal a novel connection between fundamental physics and statistical learning theory, and have implications for real-world sensing experiments.
