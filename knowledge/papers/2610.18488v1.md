# Recalibrating Many Questions with Few Labels: Minimax Rates and Identification Limits

<summary><strong>中文摘要</strong></summary>

许多相关的预测问题（我们称之为“问题”）各自只有少量标签。它们之间可以共享什么信息，以及要做出好的决策究竟需要什么？我们针对重校准同时回答这两个问题，其动机来自诸如Jev（TypeSafe AI, 2026）之类的决策模型，该模型会为开发者所写的任何是/否问题返回一个概率。一个预测器在$m$个问题上报告概率$P$，其输出律为$\mu$，每个问题有$n$个标签和一个非递减映射$g_j(p)=\mathbb{P}(Y=1\mid P=p)$；复合风险是在真实映射上相对于Brier分数的超额，并在问题上取平均。在一个非递减形状（固定效应）的sup距离$τ\le 1$和全变差$V\le 2$范围内的映射上，对于每个非原子$\mu$，极小极大复合风险在通用常数范围内为$(mn)^{-2/3}+\min\{τ^2,1/n\}+\min\{τ^2,V^2,(V/n)^{2/3}\}$。一种带每问题校正的合并等渗拟合，该校正一次性在所有问题的留出标签上确定，在不知道$τ$、$V$或$\mu$的情况下即可达到该风险（$n\ge 2$，$m\ge 3$）；当映射相差常数且$m^2/n$很大时，用每个问题自己的标签对其进行调优会损失一个阶为$\min\{n,\log(m^2/n)\}$的因子。当这些映射来自一个未知先验（随机效应）时，其他问题不必识别出贝叶斯规则。该损失，即识别遗憾，一旦某个问题的数据分布已知，至多为$2^{-n}$，这扩展了Skibinsky（1968）关于硬币的界；而在最坏情况下，对每个有限$m$至少为$β_\star^{-n}/(72\sqrt{n})$，其中$β_\star\approx 3.583$。在每个问题只有少量标签之外，无法学习到的东西几乎是不必要的。对于常数映射（一个无信息预测器）和常数映射先验，指数底数恰好是$β_\star$，而不是矩范围所暗示的4；对于单调映射，它位于$[2,β_\star]$中。

<summary><strong>英文摘要</strong></summary>

Many related prediction problems, which we call questions, come with few labels each. What information can be shared across them, and what is actually necessary for good decisions? We answer both for recalibration, motivated by decision models such as Jev (TypeSafe AI, 2026) that return a probability for any yes/no question a developer writes. A predictor reports a probability $P$ with output law $μ$ on $m$ questions, each with $n$ labels and a nondecreasing map $g_j(p)=\mathbb{P}(Y=1\mid P=p)$; the compound risk is the excess Brier score over the true maps, averaged over questions. Over maps within sup-distance $τ\le 1$ and total variation $V\le 2$ of one nondecreasing shape (fixed effects), the minimax compound risk is $(mn)^{-2/3}+\min\{τ^2,1/n\}+\min\{τ^2,V^2,(V/n)^{2/3}\}$ up to universal constants, for every non-atomic $μ$. A pooled isotonic fit with a per-question correction sized once, on held-out labels of all questions, attains it without knowing $τ$, $V$ or $μ$ ($n\ge 2$, $m\ge 3$); tuning each question on its own labels loses a factor of order $\min\{n,\log(m^2/n)\}$ when the maps differ by constants and $m^2/n$ is large. When the maps are drawn from an unknown prior (random effects), other questions need not identify the Bayes rule. The loss, the identification regret, is at most $2^{-n}$ once the law of a question's data is known, extending a bound of Skibinsky (1968) for coins, and in the worst case at least $β_\star^{-n}/(72\sqrt{n})$ at every finite $m$, where $β_\star\approx 3.583$. Beyond a few labels per question, what cannot be learned is nearly unnecessary. For constant maps (an uninformative predictor) and constant-map priors, the exponential base is exactly $β_\star$, not the 4 suggested by the moment range; for monotone maps it lies in $[2,β_\star]$.
